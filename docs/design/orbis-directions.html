Orbis · interface audit

Five directions out of the card soup

Orbis today renders almost every piece of content inside the same rounded, shadowed box — on Home, in Finance, around the AI. Five directions below, each one a complete position on how a personal intelligence app should look. Every mockup uses the app's real content: the live weather tile, the empty finance state, the workbook advice with its cited observations.

What the audit found

Hierarchy

Nineteen card classes, one visual weight

.metric-card, .stack-card, .coach-card, .currency-card, .groww-card, .rings-card and thirteen more all resolve to roughly the same radius, border and shadow. When every block is lifted, nothing is lifted — the eye has no entry point on any screen.

Consistency

Two design languages, switched by tab

Home and Invest float custom cards on a tinted ground. Finance and Health run .screen-body.grouped with iOS inset lists (.apple-list, .apple-section). Moving between tabs feels like moving between two apps that share a nav bar.

Structure

Cards inside cards inside cards

.workbook-advisor → .workbook-preview → .workbook-sheet → .workbook-advice puts four rounded containers between the screen edge and a sentence of advice. The nesting reads as indecision about what the unit of content is.

Colour

One accent carries links, icons, status and CTAs

--accent: #0b6e69 marks metric captions, section actions, primary buttons and success states at once, so colour can no longer signal this one matters. Semantic status has no separate voice.

Product

The AI is a feature in a box, not the spine

Orbis brief, workbook advice and portfolio suggestions each sit inside their own card, below the fold, behind a consent checkbox. The most distinctive thing the product does looks like the least important thing on the screen.

Empty state

A new account renders eight identical boxes saying "not added"

Empty states use the same card treatment as full ones, so the first-run screen has the density of a full dashboard and none of the information. Emptiness should be quiet, not furniture.

Tokens

Two token systems mid-migration

46 --sys-* variables now coexist with the original --bg/--ink/--card set. Whichever direction you pick, finish on one set — the migration is most of the work either way.

Ledger

No containers at all. Orbis reads as a printed statement of your life: hairline rules, figures set in a serif and aligned in a column, advice written as an argument with numbered evidence. The authority comes from typography, not from boxes.

Colour
TypeFraunces for figures and headings, Instrument Sans for prose, tabular numerals throughout
LayoutOne column, rules instead of cards, values right-aligned on a shared axis

What it replaces

All 19 card classes collapse into rules and spacing. .metric-card becomes a row; .stack-card becomes a labelled group.

Where the AI lives

Advice is the page, not a panel inside one. Evidence IDs you already return become real footnotes.

Risk

Needs disciplined copy — a serif statement exposes vague sentences. Charts have to earn their place.

Console

Conversation is the interface. The home screen is a thread Orbis has already started, the data appears as the result of visible work ("read 32 transactions"), and consent is a permission prompt in the flow rather than a checkbox in a card. This is the direction that most reads as an AI product.

Colour
TypeIBM Plex Mono for labels, chips and numbers; Instrument Sans for everything Orbis says
LayoutSingle thread, fixed ask bar, facts as inline rows inside the answer

What it replaces

Tab screens become views the thread can open. .workbook-consent becomes an inline permission, which is also a clearer disclosure.

What it needs

A real ask endpoint and routing from question to screen — the biggest build of the five, and the biggest change to what Orbis is.

Risk

A thread is bad at at-a-glance state. Keep the fact rows so the answer to "how am I doing" doesn't require typing.

Signal

A terminal for one person. White ground, hairline grid, every row a measurement with its trend drawn beside it. Colour is spent only on data — nothing else in the interface is allowed to be coloured — and AI output arrives as a bordered block with its provenance on the frame.

Colour
TypeArchivo, tight and tabular; 10px letterspaced caps for every label
LayoutFull-bleed rows on a 1px grid, values right-aligned, sparkline between name and value

What it replaces

Home stops being tiles and becomes one scan column. .chart-card and .rings-card shrink into inline sparklines.

Where it shines

Once Gmail, Groww and Apple Health are all connected, this is the only direction that shows everything at once without scrolling.

Risk

Brutal with empty data — half the rows say "no data" on day one. Needs a genuinely different first-run screen.

Field

One thing at a time. A soft ground, a single lifted surface carrying the one decision worth making today, and everything else demoted to a quiet list underneath. The opposite of a dashboard: the app decides what matters so you don't have to scan.

Colour
TypeFigtree throughout — one humanist face, three weights, no display face
LayoutGradient field, one focal surface, hairline list below, nothing else lifted

What it replaces

The metric grid stops being four equal tiles. composeBrief() stops being a sentence in a card and becomes the screen's whole top half.

What it needs

Real ranking logic: something has to decide the one focal item each day, and be right often enough to be trusted.

Risk

Hides detail by design. Power users who want the numbers will need a second, denser view behind it.

Grouped

Finish the migration you already started. Everything becomes an inset list on a grey ground — the pattern Finance and Health are half-way to — with one exception made on purpose: the AI row, which carries a slow sheen so the one generative thing on screen is also the one moving thing.

Colour
TypeSystem face (SF on iOS), 13–30px, weights 400/600/700 only
LayoutLarge title, inset lists with 0.5px separators, section captions in sentence case

What it replaces

The custom card classes go; .apple-list and .apple-section become the only containers, already written for Finance and Health.

Why it's cheapest

Home and Invest move to a pattern the codebase already has. No new type system, and dark mode comes almost free from the --sys-* tokens.

Risk

It looks like iOS, not like Orbis. The AI sheen is the only thing that distinguishes the product from Settings.

How to read these. They are not five coats of paint on the same screen — each one changes what Home is: a statement, a thread, a terminal, a single decision, or a settings list. Pick by answering one question: when someone opens Orbis with nothing connected, what should they see?

Cheapest to build: Grouped, then Ledger. Most distinctive: Console, then Signal. Best fit for the data you actually have today (mostly empty, one weather call, an occasional workbook): Field or Ledger — both are designed for a quiet screen, while Signal needs connected accounts to look like anything.

Every mockup is static HTML in this page — the real screens would keep your existing Supabase data flow, server actions and consent copy untouched. The work is globals.css and the container markup.
